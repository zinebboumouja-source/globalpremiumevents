Context and rationale

Men's mental health, a public health and leadership issue

According to the World Health Organization, more than 700,000 people died by suicide in 2019, one in every 100 deaths worldwide. The suicide rate among men is more than twice that of women: 12.6 per 100,000 compared with 5.4.[1]

In Morocco, the Economic, Social and Environmental Council (CESE) reports, based on the national survey on mental disorders, that 48.9% of the surveyed population aged 15 and over show or have shown signs of a mental disorder, while the country has only 454 psychiatrists.[2]

In this context, the role of leaders deserves particular attention: their decisions affect entire teams, and their own relationship with mental health shapes the culture of their organisations. On the occasion of International Men's Day, Leading Well opens this conversation with leaders, employers and experts.

This initiative is aligned with the United Nations 2030 Agenda and contributes to target 3.4 of Sustainable Development Goal 3: promoting mental health and well-being.[3]
